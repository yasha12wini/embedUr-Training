# Role of the Pairwise Master Key (PMK) in the 4-Way Handshake

## Introduction

The Pairwise Master Key (PMK) is one of the most important components in WPA/WPA2/WPA3 security architecture. It forms the cryptographic foundation used during the 4-way handshake to derive secure session keys.

The PMK itself is never transmitted over the air.

---

# What is PMK?

PMK stands for Pairwise Master Key.

It is a long-lived master key shared between:
- The client (STA)
- The Access Point (AP)

The PMK is used to derive temporary encryption keys.

---

# PMK Generation

## WPA/WPA2-Personal

In Personal mode:
- PMK is derived from the Wi-Fi passphrase using PBKDF2.

Formula:

```text
PMK = PBKDF2(passphrase, SSID)
```

---

## WPA/WPA2-Enterprise

In Enterprise mode:
- PMK is generated after successful 802.1X/EAP authentication.

The RADIUS server helps establish the PMK securely.

---

# Why PMK is Important

The PMK acts as the root secret used for:
- Mutual authentication
- Session key derivation
- Encryption key generation

Without the PMK:
- Secure communication cannot be established

---

# PMK in the 4-Way Handshake

The PMK is used to derive the PTK (Pairwise Transient Key).

---

# PTK Derivation

The PTK is generated using:
- PMK
- ANonce (AP nonce)
- SNonce (STA nonce)
- AP MAC address
- STA MAC address

Formula Concept:

```text
PTK = PRF(PMK, ANonce, SNonce, MAC addresses)
```

---

# Purpose of PTK

The PTK contains:
- Encryption keys
- Integrity keys
- MIC keys

It secures unicast communication between AP and client.

---

# Why PMK is Never Transmitted

The PMK remains local to both devices.

Advantages:
- Reduces exposure risk
- Prevents direct interception
- Improves overall security

Only derived values and MIC validations are exchanged.

---

# PMK and Mutual Authentication

During the handshake:
- Both AP and client prove possession of the same PMK
- MIC verification confirms legitimacy

If PMKs differ:
- Handshake fails

---

# Security Benefits

## 1. Secure Key Hierarchy
PMK acts as the root key.

## 2. Session Isolation
Temporary PTKs are generated per session.

## 3. Reduced Exposure
Master key never traverses the network.

## 4. Replay Protection
Nonce usage prevents replay attacks.

---

# Practical Flow

## Step 1
Client authenticates and derives PMK.

## Step 2
AP sends ANonce.

## Step 3
Client generates SNonce and PTK.

## Step 4
AP independently derives same PTK.

## Step 5
MIC validation confirms PMK possession.

---

# What Happens if PMKs Do Not Match?

Possible causes:
- Wrong password
- Authentication failure
- Corrupted key derivation

Result:
- Handshake failure
- Connection denied

---

# Key Takeaways

- PMK is the master cryptographic secret.
- PMK is used to derive PTK during the 4-way handshake.
- PMK is never transmitted over the air.
- Both AP and STA must possess the same PMK.
- PMK enables secure encrypted communication.
