# Power Saving Mechanisms in the Wi-Fi MAC Layer

## Introduction

Power saving is an important function of the IEEE 802.11 MAC layer, especially for:
- Mobile devices
- Smartphones
- IoT devices
- Battery-powered systems

The MAC layer provides mechanisms that allow devices to reduce power consumption while maintaining connectivity.

---

# Why Power Saving is Needed

Wireless interfaces consume significant power because they:
- Continuously monitor the channel
- Receive beacon frames
- Perform transmissions and scanning

Without power-saving mechanisms:
- Battery life decreases rapidly

---

# Basic Power Save Concept

A station alternates between:
- Awake state
- Sleep state

The AP buffers packets for sleeping clients.

---

# Legacy Power Save Mechanism

## Basic Operation

1. Client informs AP it is entering sleep mode
2. AP buffers frames
3. AP indicates buffered traffic in beacon TIM
4. Client wakes periodically
5. Client retrieves buffered packets

---

# TIM (Traffic Indication Map)

Beacon frames contain:
- TIM bitmap

TIM indicates:
- Which clients have buffered traffic pending

---

# DTIM (Delivery TIM)

DTIM is used for:
- Broadcast/multicast delivery scheduling

Clients wake during DTIM intervals to receive multicast traffic.

---

# PS-Poll Mechanism

The client sends:
- PS-Poll frame

Purpose:
- Request buffered packets from AP

---

# U-APSD (Unscheduled Automatic Power Save Delivery)

Introduced for:
- WMM power saving

Benefits:
- Reduced signaling overhead
- Better VoIP support

Common in:
- Smartphones
- Voice applications

---

# Target Wake Time (TWT)

Introduced in Wi-Fi 6.

Allows:
- Scheduled wake/sleep negotiation

Benefits:
- Massive IoT scalability
- Better battery efficiency

---

# TWT Advantages

## Reduced Contention
Devices wake at scheduled times.

## Better Battery Life
Clients sleep longer.

## Improved Efficiency
AP schedules traffic intelligently.

---

# Comparison of Power Save Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Legacy PS | Basic sleep/wake |
| PS-Poll | Retrieve buffered frames |
| U-APSD | Efficient QoS power save |
| TWT | Scheduled communication |
