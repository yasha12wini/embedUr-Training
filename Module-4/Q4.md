# Wi-Fi Scanning Process and Its Types

## Introduction

Scanning is the process through which a wireless client discovers nearby Access Points (APs).

Before association can occur:
- The client must identify available networks
- Gather capability information
- Evaluate signal quality

Scanning is one of the first steps in Wi-Fi connectivity.

---

# Objectives of Scanning

Scanning helps clients:
- Discover available SSIDs
- Identify supported capabilities
- Measure signal strength
- Determine channel information
- Select the best AP

---

# Types of Scanning

There are two major scanning methods:
1. Passive Scanning
2. Active Scanning

---

# 1. Passive Scanning

## Definition

In passive scanning:
- The client listens for beacon frames from APs.

The client does not actively transmit probe requests.

---

## Beacon Frames

APs periodically broadcast beacon frames containing:
- SSID
- Supported rates
- Security information
- TIM
- Capabilities

---

## Passive Scanning Process

1. Client tunes to a channel
2. Waits for beacon frames
3. Collects AP information
4. Moves to next channel
5. Repeats process

---

## Advantages

- Lower network overhead
- Reduced interference
- Mandatory in some regulatory domains

---

## Disadvantages

- Slower discovery process
- Depends on beacon interval timing

---

# 2. Active Scanning

## Definition

In active scanning:
- The client actively sends probe request frames.

APs respond with probe responses.

---

## Active Scanning Process

1. Client switches to a channel
2. Sends probe request
3. AP responds with probe response
4. Client collects information
5. Client moves to next channel

---

## Types of Probe Requests

### Broadcast Probe
Requests responses from all APs.

### Directed Probe
Targets specific SSID.

---

## Advantages

- Faster discovery
- More efficient roaming

---

## Disadvantages

- Additional airtime usage
- Increased RF traffic

---

# Comparison Between Passive and Active Scanning

| Feature | Passive | Active |
|---|---|---|
| AP Discovery | Beacon listening | Probe requests |
| Speed | Slower | Faster |
| Airtime Usage | Lower | Higher |
| Client Transmission | No | Yes |

---

# Channel Scanning Approaches

## Full Scan
Client scans all channels.

## Partial Scan
Client scans selected channels.

## Neighbor-Assisted Scan
Uses 802.11k neighbor reports.

---

# Scanning During Roaming

When signal quality drops:
- Clients perform background scanning
- Candidate APs are evaluated

Fast scanning is critical for:
- VoWiFi
- Real-time applications

---

# Enterprise Importance

Efficient scanning improves:
- Roaming speed
- AP selection
- User experience

Modern WLAN optimization often focuses heavily on scanning efficiency.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Scanning is the first step in Wi-Fi connectivity.
- Passive scanning listens for beacons.
- Active scanning uses probe requests.
- Scanning efficiency directly affects roaming performance.
