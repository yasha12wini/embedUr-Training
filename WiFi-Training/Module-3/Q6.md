
# Guard Intervals in WLAN Transmission

## Introduction

Guard Intervals (GI) are time gaps inserted between OFDM symbols in WLAN transmission to reduce Inter‑Symbol Interference (ISI) caused by multipath propagation.

In indoor wireless environments:
- Signals reflect off walls and objects
- Multiple delayed copies arrive at the receiver
- Symbol overlap occurs

Guard Intervals mitigate these issues.

---

## Multipath Propagation

Multipath occurs when:
- Signals travel through multiple paths
- Delayed signals interfere with subsequent symbols

Effects:
- Inter‑Symbol Interference
- Reduced decoding accuracy
- Increased packet errors

---

## Purpose of Guard Interval

Guard Interval acts as a buffer period:
- Absorbs delayed reflections
- Preserves OFDM orthogonality
- Prevents symbol overlap

In OFDM systems, the GI is implemented using a Cyclic Prefix.

---

## Cyclic Prefix

The cyclic prefix copies the end portion of the OFDM symbol and places it at the beginning.

Benefits:
- Simplifies equalization
- Maintains orthogonality
- Improves robustness against delay spread

---

## Standard Guard Interval Values

| Standard | Normal GI | Short GI |
|---|---|---|
| 802.11a/g | 0.8 µs | Not used |
| 802.11n | 0.8 µs | 0.4 µs |
| 802.11ac | 0.8 µs | 0.4 µs |
| 802.11ax | 0.8/1.6/3.2 µs | Flexible |

---

## Short Guard Interval (SGI)

Short GI reduces guard duration from:
- 800 ns → 400 ns

Advantages:
- Higher throughput
- Improved spectral efficiency

Throughput improvement:
- Approximately 10–11%

Example:
- 150 Mbps → approximately 165 Mbps

---

## Why Short GI Improves Efficiency

OFDM transmission time consists of:
- Useful symbol duration
- Guard interval overhead

Reducing GI:
- Reduces overhead
- Increases useful transmission percentage

Formula:
Efficiency = Useful Symbol Time / Total Symbol Time

Smaller GI:
- Higher efficiency
- Better PHY rate

---

## Limitations of Short GI

Short GI is effective only when:
- Delay spread is small
- Multipath reflections are limited
- SNR is good

Problems:
- Higher ISI risk
- Reduced robustness in complex RF environments

---

## Guard Intervals in Wi‑Fi 6

Wi‑Fi 6 introduced flexible GI durations:
- 0.8 µs
- 1.6 µs
- 3.2 µs

Benefits:
- Better outdoor support
- Improved OFDMA performance
- Enhanced long‑range operation

---

## Enterprise WLAN Considerations

WLAN engineers select GI based on:
- Environment
- AP density
- Delay spread
- Client capabilities

Dense indoor deployments:
- Often use SGI

Industrial or outdoor deployments:
- Prefer longer GI

---

## Conclusion

Guard Intervals are critical for reliable OFDM communication in WLAN systems. They reduce Inter‑Symbol Interference caused by multipath propagation. Short Guard Intervals improve spectral efficiency and throughput but require cleaner RF conditions. Modern Wi‑Fi standards provide flexible GI configurations to optimize performance across different deployment scenarios.
