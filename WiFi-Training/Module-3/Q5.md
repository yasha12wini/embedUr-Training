
# Wi‑Fi Frequency Bands and Channel Allocation

## Introduction

Wi‑Fi operates in unlicensed ISM and U‑NII frequency bands allocated by regulatory bodies such as the FCC, ETSI, and TRAI. These bands are divided into channels that allow multiple WLAN networks to coexist.

The primary Wi‑Fi bands are:
- 2.4 GHz
- 5 GHz
- 6 GHz

---

## 2.4 GHz Band

Frequency Range:
- Approximately 2.400 GHz to 2.4835 GHz

Characteristics:
- Longer range
- Better wall penetration
- Higher interference
- Limited spectrum

Channel Width:
- 20 MHz primarily
- 40 MHz optional in newer standards

### Channels in 2.4 GHz

| Channel | Center Frequency |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2.412 GHz |
| 6 | 2.437 GHz |
| 11 | 2.462 GHz |

Important:
- Channels overlap heavily
- Only channels 1, 6, and 11 are non‑overlapping in most regions

Applications:
- Legacy devices
- IoT devices
- Long‑range deployments

---

## 5 GHz Band

Frequency Range:
- Approximately 5.150 GHz to 5.825 GHz

Advantages:
- More available channels
- Lower interference
- Higher throughput
- Better support for wide channels

Disadvantages:
- Shorter range
- Reduced penetration

---

## 5 GHz U‑NII Bands

| U‑NII Band | Frequency Range |
|---|---|
| U‑NII‑1 | 5.150–5.250 GHz |
| U‑NII‑2 | 5.250–5.350 GHz |
| U‑NII‑2e | 5.470–5.725 GHz |
| U‑NII‑3 | 5.725–5.825 GHz |

---

## DFS Channels

Dynamic Frequency Selection (DFS) channels:
- Detect radar systems
- Vacate channels when radar is detected
- Improve spectrum availability

DFS is mandatory in many regulatory regions.

---

## 6 GHz Band (Wi‑Fi 6E and Wi‑Fi 7)

Frequency Range:
- Approximately 5.925 GHz to 7.125 GHz

Advantages:
- Massive spectrum availability
- Lower congestion
- High throughput
- Low latency

Features:
- Supports 160 MHz and 320 MHz channels
- Ideal for Wi‑Fi 7

---

## Channel Widths

Modern Wi‑Fi supports multiple channel widths.

| Channel Width | Usage |
|---|---|
| 20 MHz | Basic operation |
| 40 MHz | Moderate throughput |
| 80 MHz | High throughput |
| 160 MHz | Very high throughput |
| 320 MHz | Wi‑Fi 7 ultra‑high throughput |

Wider channels:
- Increase data rate
- Reduce number of available channels
- Increase interference probability

---

## Channel Bonding

Channel bonding combines adjacent channels.

Example:
- Two 20 MHz channels → 40 MHz
- Four 20 MHz channels → 80 MHz

Benefits:
- Increased PHY data rate

Drawbacks:
- Higher interference sensitivity

---

## Regulatory Considerations

Different countries permit different channels and power levels.

Key constraints:
- EIRP limits
- DFS requirements
- Indoor/outdoor restrictions

WLAN engineers must comply with regional regulations.

---

## Channel Reuse

Enterprise WLAN deployments use:
- Frequency reuse
- Power optimization
- Directional antennas
- Channel planning

Goals:
- Minimize co‑channel interference
- Maximize spectral efficiency

---

## Comparison of Wi‑Fi Bands

| Feature | 2.4 GHz | 5 GHz | 6 GHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| Range | High | Medium | Lower |
| Interference | High | Medium | Low |
| Throughput | Lower | High | Very High |
| Channel Availability | Limited | Large | Massive |
| Penetration | Excellent | Good | Moderate |

---

## Conclusion

Wi‑Fi frequency bands and channels are fundamental to WLAN design. Modern enterprise networks increasingly rely on 5 GHz and 6 GHz bands due to their higher throughput, reduced interference, and greater channel availability. Proper channel planning and RF management are essential for achieving optimal WLAN performance.
