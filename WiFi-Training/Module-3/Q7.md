
# Structure of an IEEE 802.11 PHY Layer Frame

## Introduction

In IEEE 802.11 WLAN systems, the PHY layer frame is known as the PPDU (PLCP Protocol Data Unit). The PHY frame encapsulates MAC layer data and prepares it for wireless transmission.

The PHY frame structure ensures:
- Synchronization
- Channel estimation
- Error detection
- Data recovery

---

## PHY Layer Architecture

The PHY layer consists of:
- PLCP (Physical Layer Convergence Procedure)
- PMD (Physical Medium Dependent)

Responsibilities:
- PLCP: framing and signaling
- PMD: modulation and transmission

---

## General PHY Frame Structure

A PHY frame generally contains:

1. Preamble
2. PHY Header
3. PSDU/Data Payload
4. Tail bits
5. Padding

---

## 1. Preamble

The preamble is transmitted first.

Functions:
- Synchronization
- Timing recovery
- Frequency offset estimation
- Channel estimation

Components:
- Short Training Field (STF)
- Long Training Field (LTF)

---

## Short Training Field (STF)

Purpose:
- Signal detection
- AGC convergence
- Coarse synchronization

Characteristics:
- Repeated patterns
- Fast receiver locking

---

## Long Training Field (LTF)

Purpose:
- Channel estimation
- Fine frequency synchronization

Important for:
- OFDM equalization
- MIMO spatial stream decoding

---

## 2. PHY Header

PHY header contains transmission parameters.

Typical fields:
- Modulation scheme
- Coding rate
- Packet length
- MCS index
- Spatial stream information

The receiver reads the PHY header before decoding payload data.

---

## 3. PSDU (PHY Service Data Unit)

PSDU contains:
- MAC frame
- User payload
- Control information

The PSDU is:
- Encoded
- Interleaved
- Modulated
- Mapped onto OFDM subcarriers

---

## 4. Tail Bits

Tail bits:
- Reset convolutional encoder state
- Improve decoding accuracy

Typically:
- 6 bits

---

## 5. Padding Bits

Padding ensures:
- OFDM symbol alignment
- Integer symbol boundaries

---

## OFDM Symbol Mapping

After encoding:
- Bits are mapped to modulation symbols
- Symbols assigned to subcarriers
- IFFT generates time‑domain waveform

---

## PHY Frame in Different Wi‑Fi Generations

### 802.11a/g

Uses:
- Legacy OFDM preamble
- Single user transmission

---

### 802.11n

Adds:
- HT preamble
- Multiple spatial streams
- MIMO training fields

---

### 802.11ac

Introduces:
- VHT preamble
- MU‑MIMO support
- Beamforming information

---

### 802.11ax

Adds:
- HE preamble
- OFDMA signaling
- Resource Unit allocation
- Uplink scheduling

---

## Importance of PHY Framing

Proper PHY framing enables:
- Reliable synchronization
- Efficient decoding
- Backward compatibility
- Multi‑user communication

---

## Conclusion

The IEEE 802.11 PHY layer frame is carefully structured to ensure reliable wireless communication under complex RF conditions. Components such as preambles, training fields, PHY headers, and PSDUs enable synchronization, channel estimation, and efficient payload delivery. As Wi‑Fi standards evolved, PHY frames became increasingly sophisticated to support MIMO, OFDMA, beamforming, and ultra‑high throughput communication.
