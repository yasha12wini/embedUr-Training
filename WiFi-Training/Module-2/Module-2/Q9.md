# Question 9
## What happens to wireless clients connected to a Lightweight AP in local mode if the WLC goes down?

# Answer

In local mode, lightweight APs depend heavily on the WLC for control and client traffic forwarding.

When the WLC goes down, client behavior depends on the AP configuration and WLAN architecture.

---

# Local Mode Operation

In local mode:
- AP tunnels client traffic to WLC
- WLC performs centralized switching
- AP depends on controller communication

---

# What Happens When WLC Fails

## 1. Existing Clients
Existing wireless clients may temporarily continue communication until:
- CAPWAP tunnel timeout occurs
- Session state expires

Eventually:
- Clients disconnect
- Traffic forwarding stops

---

## 2. New Client Connections
New associations typically fail because:
- Authentication services are unavailable
- AP cannot complete WLAN control operations

---

## 3. CAPWAP Tunnel Failure
AP detects:
- Missing keepalive responses
- Tunnel failure
- Loss of controller connectivity

---

# AP Recovery Behavior

After failure detection:
1. AP attempts reconnect.
2. Searches for backup WLC.
3. May reboot depending on configuration.

---

# Impact on Network

| Component | Effect |
|---|---|
| Existing Clients | Eventually disconnected |
| New Clients | Cannot join |
| Roaming | Fails |
| Central Policies | Unavailable |

---

# Solutions

Enterprise networks commonly use:
- Redundant WLCs
- HA failover
- FlexConnect local switching

These improve survivability.

---

# Conclusion

In local mode, lightweight APs are highly dependent on the WLC. If the controller fails, client connectivity is significantly affected unless redundancy or FlexConnect mechanisms are implemented.
