# What Happens if a Wrong Passphrase is Used During the 4-Way Handshake

## Introduction

In WPA/WPA2-Personal networks, both the client and access point derive the Pairwise Master Key (PMK) from the Wi-Fi passphrase.

If the client enters the wrong passphrase:
- The PMK generated by the client differs from the AP’s PMK.
- The 4-way handshake fails.

---

# Key Concept

The 4-way handshake does not directly verify the password.

Instead, it verifies whether:
- Both devices possess the same PMK.

---

# PMK Derivation

Both AP and client independently derive the PMK.

Formula:

```text
PMK = PBKDF2(passphrase, SSID)
```

If the passphrase differs:
- PMKs become different.

---

# What Happens During the Handshake

## Step 1: AP Sends ANonce

The AP sends:
- ANonce
- Replay counter

The client receives this information.

---

## Step 2: Client Generates PTK

The client:
- Uses its locally generated PMK
- Uses ANonce and SNonce
- Derives PTK

However:
- Since the password is wrong,
- The PMK is incorrect,
- Therefore PTK becomes incorrect.

---

## Step 3: Client Sends MIC

The client sends:
- SNonce
- MIC

The MIC is computed using the incorrect PTK.

---

## Step 4: AP Validates MIC

The AP:
- Uses its correct PMK
- Derives the correct PTK
- Verifies the MIC

Result:
- MIC verification fails.

---

# Final Outcome

Since MIC validation fails:
- AP rejects the handshake
- Connection fails
- Client cannot join the network

---

# User-Side Symptoms

Typical symptoms include:
- “Unable to connect”
- “Incorrect password”
- Continuous reconnect attempts
- Authentication timeout

---

# Internal Protocol Behavior

The AP may:
- Send deauthentication frames
- Log MIC failures
- Increase replay counters
- Terminate association

---

# Security Importance

This behavior is essential because:
- Attackers without the correct password cannot derive valid keys
- Unauthorized devices are blocked

The AP never needs to send the actual password over the air.

---

# Why Attackers Capture Handshakes

Attackers often capture 4-way handshakes because:
- They can attempt offline dictionary attacks
- They guess passwords locally

If a guessed password generates matching MIC values:
- Password is correct

This is why strong passwords are critical.

---

# WPA3 Improvement

WPA3 uses SAE instead of PSK-based authentication.

Benefits:
- Better protection against offline attacks
- Improved password security

---

# Key Takeaways

- Wrong passphrases generate incorrect PMKs.
- Incorrect PMKs generate invalid PTKs.
- MIC verification fails during the handshake.
- The AP rejects the connection.
- The 4-way handshake protects the network from unauthorized access.
