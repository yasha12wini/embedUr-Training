# Question 1
## What are the different 802.11 PHY layer standards? Compare their characteristics.

# Answer

The IEEE 802.11 Physical (PHY) layer defines how wireless signals are transmitted over the air. Over time, multiple PHY standards were introduced to improve throughput, range, spectral efficiency, reliability, and latency.

---

# Major 802.11 PHY Standards

| Standard | Wi‑Fi Generation | Frequency Band | Maximum Data Rate | Channel Width | Key Technology |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 802.11 | Legacy | 2.4 GHz | 2 Mbps | 20 MHz | FHSS / DSSS |
| 802.11b | Wi‑Fi 1 | 2.4 GHz | 11 Mbps | 20 MHz | DSSS |
| 802.11a | Wi‑Fi 2 | 5 GHz | 54 Mbps | 20 MHz | OFDM |
| 802.11g | Wi‑Fi 3 | 2.4 GHz | 54 Mbps | 20 MHz | OFDM |
| 802.11n | Wi‑Fi 4 | 2.4/5 GHz | 600 Mbps | 20/40 MHz | MIMO + OFDM |
| 802.11ac | Wi‑Fi 5 | 5 GHz | 6.9 Gbps | Up to 160 MHz | MU‑MIMO |
| 802.11ax | Wi‑Fi 6/6E | 2.4/5/6 GHz | 9.6 Gbps | Up to 160 MHz | OFDMA + MU‑MIMO |
| 802.11be | Wi‑Fi 7 | 2.4/5/6 GHz | 40+ Gbps | Up to 320 MHz | Multi‑Link Operation |

---

# Evolution of PHY Technologies

## 1. 802.11 and 802.11b
- Used DSSS and FHSS techniques.
- Operated only in the 2.4 GHz ISM band.
- Suitable for low‑speed wireless communication.
- Susceptible to interference due to crowded spectrum.

## 2. 802.11a and 802.11g
- Introduced OFDM.
- Improved spectral efficiency significantly.
- Enabled higher throughput up to 54 Mbps.
- 802.11a operated at 5 GHz, while 802.11g used 2.4 GHz.

## 3. 802.11n
- Introduced MIMO technology.
- Allowed multiple spatial streams.
- Supported channel bonding (40 MHz).
- Greatly improved range and throughput.

## 4. 802.11ac
- Introduced MU‑MIMO.
- Supported wider channels (80/160 MHz).
- Improved beamforming.
- Operated only in the 5 GHz band.

## 5. 802.11ax
- Introduced OFDMA.
- Improved efficiency in dense deployments.
- Reduced latency.
- Improved power efficiency using Target Wake Time (TWT).

## 6. 802.11be
- Introduces Extremely High Throughput (EHT).
- Supports 320 MHz channels.
- Uses Multi‑Link Operation (MLO).
- Designed for AR/VR, AI workloads, and ultra‑low latency communication.

# Comparison Summary

| Feature | Older Standards | Modern Standards |
|---|---|---|
| Access Method | DSSS/FHSS | OFDM/OFDMA |
| Multiple Antennas | No | MIMO/MU‑MIMO |
| Spectrum Efficiency | Low | Very High |
| Maximum Throughput | Few Mbps | Multi‑Gbps |
| Latency | Higher | Lower |
| Interference Handling | Weak | Strong |
| Dense User Performance | Poor | Excellent |

---

# Conclusion

The evolution of 802.11 PHY standards reflects the continuous improvement of wireless communication systems. Modern Wi‑Fi generations achieve higher throughput, better spectral efficiency, lower latency, and improved reliability using technologies such as OFDM, OFDMA, MIMO, and MU‑MIMO.
